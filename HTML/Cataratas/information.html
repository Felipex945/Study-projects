General information

Iguaçu Falls (Spanish: Cataratas del Iguazú) is a group of approximately 275 waterfalls on the Iguaçu River (in the Paraná River Basin), located between Iguaçu National Park, Paraná, in Brazil, and Iguazú National Park in Misiones, Argentina, on the border between the two countries. The total area of both national parks corresponds to 250 thousand hectares of subtropical forest and is considered a Natural World Heritage Site. The Argentine national park was created in 1934, while the Brazilian park was inaugurated in 1939. Both protection areas with the purpose of managing and preserving the water source that represents this cataract and the environment as a whole around it. Both Brazilian and Argentine parks became World Heritage Sites in 1984 and 1986, respectively. The geological formation of the falls is associated with volcanic processes that occurred around 500 million years ago[1] and, since 2002, Iguaçu National Park has been considered one of the Brazilian geological sites.[2] Historically, the first European to find Iguazu Falls was the Spaniard Álvar Núñez Cabeza de Vaca, in 1541.[3] Currently, it is the second most visited place by foreigners in Brazil.[4] In rainy seasons, Iguazu Falls is the 3rd largest in the world in terms of water volume. Its flow increases 10 times,[5] reaching 11.3 thousand cubic meters per second, when the normal is 1.5 thousand.[6] Iguazu Falls participated in the global campaign to choose the seven natural wonders of the world, organized by the New 7 Wonders Foundation. The falls were among the 28 finalists in the campaign, which lasted until the end of 2011 when the number of 1 billion votes was reached.[7] On January 31, 2012, Google prepared a special doodle in honor of the discovery of Iguazu Falls by Álvar Núñez Cabeza de Vaca.
